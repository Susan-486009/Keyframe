Honest Catch-Up Policy

Missed an immunisation date? You have not ruined your child's protection.

Life happens: market days, travel to the village, illness, lack of transport fare, or a lost paper card. The single biggest mistake is staying away out of shame or fear of nurse rebuke. Routine vaccines do not expire inside the body. Here is what you need to know to return safely.

01

You NEVER Start Over

If your baby took Penta 1 at 6 weeks and missed clinic for three months, you do not start from birth again. You simply receive Penta 2. The immune system remembers previous doses forever.

02

Vaccines Are 100% Free

Federal government routine vaccines supplied by NPHCDA are free at every public primary healthcare centre. No nurse is legally permitted to charge for the vaccine ampoule or syringe.

03

A Lost Card Can Be Replaced

If your yellow card was soaked in rain, torn, or misplaced during a relocation, go to the PHC anyway. The clinic maintains a master register book and will issue a replacement card.

Caregiver Script

What to say at the triage desk when you are late

Many young mothers fear being scolded by clinic staff. Health workers want your baby protected. Practice these exact words:

"Good morning, Nurse. My baby is [Baby's Age in months]. We had travel / emergency and missed our appointment date on the card. I brought my baby today so we can receive our next catch-up dose."

If the card is lost: Add: "Our paper card was misplaced, but my child took the 6-week vaccines here. Please help me check your registry register."
Official Catch-Up Protocol

How missed doses are handled by clinic age

Vaccine Routine Window If Missed: Catch-Up Rule
BCG (Tuberculosis)
Birth to 2 weeks
Can be given up to 11 months of age if no scar is visible and child has not received it.
Pentavalent & PCV
6, 10, and 14 weeks
Resume where you stopped. Maintain at least a 4-week gap between consecutive doses. Never restart from dose 1.
Rotavirus Drops
6, 10, and 14 weeks
Must be completed before 24 months of age. If baby is older, the nurse may omit rota and proceed with other injectables.
Measles & Yellow Fever
9 Months
Can and must be given to any child under 5 years who never took them. High priority during community outbreaks.
Guidelines cross-referenced with the World Health Organization (WHO) Catch-Up Guidelines and the National Primary Health Care Development Agency (NPHCDA) field manuals.
